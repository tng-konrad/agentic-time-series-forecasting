## LinkedIn post

A routing diagram is easy to like. Testing whether its decisions help takes a little longer.

I expanded my forecasting experiment to 100 products, three stores and six forecast dates: 1,800 cases, with a separate development period to choose a fixed comparison workflow.

The same simple rule routes sales histories to regular or intermittent forecasting methods. No new LLM calls. No threshold tuning after seeing the test errors.

The result: 0.54% lower average MASE than the development-selected fixed workflow, and 27.45% fewer requested model fits than testing every candidate.

There are qualifications worth reading. Another fixed family nearly matches routing. Testing all candidates still gives the lowest average error. Individual store–date combinations can favor the comparator.

The larger experiment makes those differences visible. It also separates an auditable decision trail from the untested claim that analysts will find the forecast more explainable.

The new article walks through the controls, chronological evaluation and results. The companion notebook regenerates all five graphs and four tables.
